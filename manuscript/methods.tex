
\subsection{Reference arrays}\label{sec:m-truth}
Reference arrays came from the 142-individual Y pangenome of Hallast et al.\ \cite{Hallast2026}. For 17 incomplete arrays (9 DYZ3, 8 Yq12) from 16 individuals, we used the HPRC release 2 assembly of the same individual \cite{Lucas2026}. Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:array_structure} shows 156 assemblies, 140 of the Y pangenome and these 16. An array was eligible when it lay on one contig with at least 100 kb of flanking sequence on both sides, contained no assembly gap and had no 1-kb bin flagged by all four quality tracks (Flagger and NucFlag, each with HiFi and nanopore reads). In one male (HG01261), a 1-kb bin inside DYZ2 was flagged by all four tracks, so its DYZ2 and Yq12 arrays were excluded and its DYZ1 and DYZ18S arrays kept.

The panels contain 129 DYZ3, 90 Yq12, 91 DYZ1, 90 DYZ2, 91 DYZ18S, 139 DYZ19 and 140 DYZ17 arrays (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:array_structure}). Of these individuals, 114, 82, 83, 82, 83, 125 and 126, respectively, have high-coverage 1000 Genomes short reads \cite{ByrskaBishop2022} and form the evaluation sets.

Reference coverage is uneven across the tree. Of the 33 lineages of Fig.~\ref{fig:biology}a, 5 have no evaluated DYZ3 reference (J2a1*, J2a1a2, J2a2, O2a2b2 and O2b, 10 males). Seven have none for Yq12 (those five plus NO* and O1a, 31 males). Their estimates therefore rest on the excluded-lineage performance.

\subsection{Satellite annotation}\label{sec:m-units}
Reference lengths follow the annotation of Hallast et al.\ \cite{Hallast2026}. DYZ3 and Yq12 use the array coordinates, and DYZ19 uses the main-array span from their script \texttt{filterDYZ19HMMER.py}. DYZ1, DYZ2 and DYZ18S (DYZ18 plus the 3.1-kb repeat) are sums of the HMMER blocks retained by their motifs and script \texttt{filterYq12HMMER.py} (\url{https://github.com/Markloftus/PanYChromosome}) \cite{Hallast2026}. Assemblies without a published annotation, including the HPRC release 2 replacements, were annotated with the same motifs, nhmmer options and filters \cite{Hallast2026,Wheeler2013}. DYZ17 is not in that annotation: we built a profile HMM from start-to-start repeat units of 140 assemblies with MAFFT \cite{Katoh2013}, hmmbuild and hmmemit \cite{Eddy2011} (5,573-bp consensus). Hits less than 10 kb apart were joined into blocks. DYZ17 length was the span of the longest block, from its first to its last hit. Our own profile-HMM annotation of Yq12 defines only the regions from which markers are sampled.

\subsection{Marker selection}\label{sec:m-markers}
Canonical 31-mers were counted in the reference arrays with KMC 3.2.4 \cite{Kokot2017}. Candidates had to occur in at least 15\% of the assemblies for a target or component and nowhere in the non-Y sequence of CHM13 \cite{Nurk2022} or of 71 high-quality HPRC release 2 haplotypes from individuals outside the study. A marker for target or component $c$ had to occur in at least a fraction $p$ of its assemblies and outside that target or component in fewer than a fraction $o$ of the Y assemblies. We chose $p\in\{0.50,0.60,0.70,0.80,0.90,0.95\}$ and $o\in\{0,0.05,0.10,0.25\}$ for each marker set by 5-fold cross-validation inside each training partition. Here $o=0$ requires absence outside the target or component.

The single-copy Y ruler contains X-degenerate 31-mers present in at least 90\% of the training assemblies. Ruler, satellite and autosomal-window 31-mers with more than ten one-mismatch variants (median over the 72 Y-free genomes of the non-Y filter: CHM13 without chrY and 71 HPRC release 2 X haplotypes) were excluded, because sequencing errors in reads from these repeats add counts in samples without Y. In 149 assemblies, a median of 99.49\% of ruler 31-mers occurred once on the Y.

The released model contains 6,183 DYZ3, 201,987 DYZ1, 30,442 DYZ2, 28,650 DYZ18S, 91,086 DYZ19, 75,149 DYZ17 and 21,417 Yq12-residual markers, 6,860,192 ruler markers and 386,153 markers of an autosomal window at chr8:91--101 Mb (Autosomal window). It also counts 577,108 markers of the whole Yq12, which are not used for the estimate. Masked superstrings \cite{Sladky2023} store the marker set.

\subsection{Autosomal window}\label{sec:m-window}
Y dosage needs an autosomal reference with a stable copy number. We tested 2,503 autosomal windows of GRCh38. We aligned each of the 465 HPRC release 2 haplotypes to GRCh38 with minimap2 2.31 (\texttt{-x asm20 -{}-eqx -c}) \cite{Li2018}. We built an implicit pangenome graph from these alignments with IMPG 0.5.0 \cite{IMPG} to project each window into the haplotypes. We ranked the windows with equal weight on six criteria: conservation and single-copy state across the haplotypes, each in the worst superpopulation, copy-number variation in the reference males, difference in median copy number between superpopulations, agreement of Y dosage between libraries of 17 males sequenced more than once, and G+C content close to that of the ruler. We used chr8:91--101 Mb, which offered a good balance of the six criteria. Its markers are 31-mers of its projections into 101 high-quality haplotypes, present in at least 90\% of these haplotypes and at one copy in at least 95\% of the HPRC haplotypes of every superpopulation (386,153 of 450,821).

\subsection{Read counting}\label{sec:m-counting}
Yruler reads BAM, CRAM, SAM, FASTQ or FASTA files through HTSlib \cite{Bonfield2021}, including unmapped reads. Indexed BAM and CRAM files are read in parallel. Secondary and supplementary records are skipped. Duplicate reads are retained, and no mapping-quality or base-quality filter is applied. In the 1,597 males of the population analyses, the duplicate fraction (median 9.04\%, 10th--90th percentile 7.09--11.13\%) did not correlate with any satellite length within lineages ($|\rho|\le0.072$). Removing duplicate reads in 30 males reduced DYZ2 estimates by a median of 16.21\% and increased DYZ3 estimates by 8.02\%. Every canonical 31-mer occurrence adds one to its marker count. Yruler writes per-marker counts, counts summed by marker set and estimates.

\subsection{G+C correction}\label{sec:m-gc}
Libraries differ in their recovery of reads poor in G and C, which affects array estimates. Yruler derives a correction from the ruler markers in each sample. Counts above $10\times(\mathrm{mean}+1)$, where the mean is over all ruler markers, are omitted from the correction curve. For each 31-mer G+C content, relative depth is the mean retained ruler count at that content divided by the overall mean retained ruler count. Correction requires at least 1,000,000 ruler counts. A G+C bin needs at least 1,000 ruler markers and 10,000 retained counts, and other bins take the nearest qualifying value. Each satellite marker count is divided by the relative depth at its G+C content. Satellite marker sums are then multiplied by the mean relative depth over all ruler markers to keep satellite and ruler counts on the same scale. Without a qualifying curve, raw counts are used. These are fixed safety limits, the same at every read depth.

DYZ2 is rich in A and T, and its markers with few G or C bases gave the largest differences between libraries of the same male. DYZ2 markers therefore require at least 14 G or C bases of 31, a threshold chosen on ten males sequenced by both the Simons Genome Diversity Project (SGDP) and 1000 Genomes, without assembly lengths.

\subsection{Length estimation}\label{sec:m-length}
For marker set $c$ in individual $i$, the corrected marker count $S^{\mathrm{GC}}_{ic}$ is divided by $S_{iY}$, the sum of the single-copy Y marker counts. A separate factor $K_c$, calculated as the sum of training lengths for the corresponding target or component divided by the sum of their count ratios, converts the ratio into base pairs,
\begin{equation}
 \widehat L_{ic}=K_c\,\frac{S^{\mathrm{GC}}_{ic}}{S_{iY}}.
\end{equation}
Yq12 length is the sum of DYZ1, DYZ2, DYZ18S and the rest of Yq12 (other repeats and unannotated sequence), which has its own markers and $K$. The component estimates describe Yq12 repeat composition. On the same 82 males and folds, pooling predictions, this sum gave a median Yq12 error of 1.73\%, against 1.80\% for a single Yq12 marker set.
The DYZ1:DYZ2 length ratio is converted into a ratio of repeat units with the median annotated unit lengths, 3,478.9 bp for DYZ1 and 2,404.4 bp for DYZ2 \cite{Hallast2026}. Unit lengths varied little between the 91 assemblies (3,441--3,509 bp for DYZ1, 2,395--2,417 bp for DYZ2), and the median unit lengths of each lineage with at least three assemblies would change the unit ratio by at most 0.39\%.

\subsection{Y dosage and Y state}\label{sec:m-dosage}
Y dosage is
\begin{equation}
 D_i=\frac{2}{s}\,\frac{S_{iY}/n_Y}{S_{iA}/n_A},
\end{equation}
where $S_{iA}$ is the count of the chr8:91--101 Mb window and $n_Y$ and $n_A$ are the numbers of markers. The scale factor $s=0.9614$ is the median unscaled dosage $2(S_{iY}/n_Y)/(S_{iA}/n_A)$ among 1000 Genomes samples with dosage above half the cohort median.

Yruler reports no lengths for $D_i\le0.025$. This threshold exceeds the highest Y dosage among 1,970 females of the three cohorts (1,601 from the 1000 Genomes Project, 267 from the Human Genome Diversity Project (HGDP) and 102 from SGDP), observed in HGDP00247 at 0.0221 and similar to that of simulated mixtures with a 2\% male contribution. All samples use the same ruler normalization. Below this threshold, retained marker counts may still contain background signal. Because the array and the ruler scale together, a uniform loss of Y (mosaic loss) leaves the estimated length per Y unchanged within 4.6\% down to a male fraction of 0.1 at 5$\times$ (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:no_y_cut}c).

For screening, the ruler markers were also summed separately over Yp (X-degenerate blocks 1--2, 850,505 markers) and proximal Yq (blocks 3--8, 6,000,853 markers). We omitted 8,834 ruler markers without a block shared by at least 90\% of the assemblies. We also counted 31-mers unique to CHM13v2 chrY in 100-kb windows. Both profiles were divided by the chr8 window and by the median of males without flagged Y profiles (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:chrY_unusual_males}). X and pseudoautosomal region 1 and 2 (PAR1, PAR2) doses use 31-mers of the HPRC haplotypes in the same way.

Samples were flagged, in this order, for Yp depletion (Yp below 0.5, Yq within 0.1 of one), Yq loss (Yp within 0.1 of one and Yq more than 0.1 below Yp) and arm imbalance (Yp and Yq more than 0.1 apart). No male had a Yp--Yq difference between 0.083 and 0.166. Subsequent categories were PAR2 gain (PAR2 dose at least 1.25 with PAR1 within 0.1 of one), Y gain (dosage at least 1.75), intermediate gain (1.25 to 1.75) or low dosage (below 0.9). These flags are screening categories, not confirmed karyotypes. All males with Y estimates, including those with flagged profiles, were included in population summaries. The per-male table marks estimates outside the range of the evaluated assemblies (134 of 1,597 males for at least one target).

To test low Y abundance, we mixed the reads of six male--female pairs from the 1000 Genomes Project at male read fractions of 0.01, 0.02, 0.1, 0.5 and 1, and female reads alone, at 5, 1 and 0.2$\times$ autosomal depth, with three seeds each (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:no_y_cut}). At 5$\times$, the measured Y dosage differed from the expected dosage (male fraction times the Y dosage of the male) by a median of 0.0016 to 0.0025 per fraction (maximum 0.0083) and exceeded it at low fractions by the female background (median 0.0024 in female-only mixtures). Satellite estimates at 5$\times$ changed by at most 4.6\% (DYZ19) at a male fraction of 0.1, the lowest fraction above the no-Y cut. In a separate calculation without the reporting cutoff, estimates changed by up to 16.6\% (DYZ3) at 0.01.

\subsection{Accuracy evaluation}\label{sec:m-benchmark}
Accuracy was measured with ten repeats of 5-fold cross-validation, with individuals of the same recorded family kept in the same fold. Markers, thresholds and $K_c$ were fitted on the four training folds only. Array lengths were predicted for individuals in the held-out fold. The error is $100|\widehat L_i/L_i-1|$ (Table~\ref{tab:accuracy}).

In a second test, each of 18 broad Y lineages was excluded in turn from training, together with any family shared with test individuals, and predicted from the remaining references (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:lineage_validation}). For depth, test reads were thinned to 20, 15, 10, 5, 2 and 1$\times$ base coverage while training reads kept full depth (Fig.~\ref{fig:method}e, and Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:y_dosage}a for Y dosage).

Parent--offspring concordance used the 309 pairs of the recorded 1000 Genomes pedigree among males with estimates. Of these, 282 pairs have the parent in the original 2,504-sample release and the offspring in the 698-sample extension, so they also compare sequencing batches (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:pedigrees}). Y specificity used 243 female genomes held out from marker selection, with the ruler replaced by its expected sum at the female haploid depth.

Four external genomes (HG002, HG003, HG005 and an Element library), held out from all model choices, had median errors of 1.00\% for DYZ3, 1.18\% for DYZ17 and 2.46\% for DYZ19 (3 males each). As a baseline, pooling predictions, we scaled aligned read depth (BWA and DRAGEN, each divided by the depth in chrY:14.5--15.5 Mb) with one factor per target, fitted on the training males of the same folds. Median errors were similar to Yruler for DYZ3 (2.11\% and 2.07\%, against 2.11\%) and higher for every other target, for example 7.02\% and 5.66\% against 1.73\% for Yq12 and 10.28\% and 10.46\% against 2.34\% for DYZ2. Trained on the males of the original 2,504-sample release and tested on those of the 698-sample extension, Yruler had median errors of 2.24\% (DYZ3, 69 males), 1.71\% (Yq12, 52) and 1.78\% (DYZ19, 75), against 2.07\%, 1.71\% and 2.37\% for the per-male cross-validation medians.

Short DYZ19 arrays span 62.3--63.5 kb in four J2a reference chromosomes, against a median reference length of 321.7 kb. Mean absolute percentage errors across the ten cross-validation repeats were 7.04\%, 2.32\% and 5.93\% in the three J2a1a1 males, and the error was 2.86\% in a fourth male not used for training (Fig.~\ref{fig:method}c). DYZ18S shares 10,384 of its 28,650 31-mers with DYZ1 and had a median per-male cross-validation error of 3.73\% (Table~\ref{tab:accuracy}).

We checked the short DYZ19 arrays of lineage J2a1a1 in males outside the training panel. For six J2a1a1 males of the 1000 Genomes nanopore resource \cite{Gustafson2024}, we aligned the 50 kb of GRCh38 sequence on each side of the DYZ19 array (chrY:20,004,500--20,054,500 and 20,351,000--20,401,000) to their nanopore assemblies with minimap2 (-x asm5) and took the distance between the inner ends of the two flanks when both aligned in full, with mapping quality 60, to one contig. In two reference males with assemblies of the same kind (hifiasm, R10.4.1 reads), this distance was within 0.12\% of the reference array length (260,305 vs 259,997 bp and 270,102 vs 269,793 bp), whereas assemblies from R9.4.1 reads (hapdup) were 3.0--45.4\% shorter, so we used only the R10.4.1 assemblies. Four J2a1a1 males had arrays of 62,433--63,808 bp, and Yruler estimates differed by $-$1.2\% to +5.0\%. The R10.4.1 assembly of the fifth was broken in the array, and the sixth had only an R9.4.1 assembly.

\subsection{Model choices}
The inner-loop score, the DYZ2 G+C floor and the autosomal window were fixed outside the cross-validation. The score (the mean, chosen among the mean, median and 90th percentile of the inner errors) changed the mean error over the seven targets by at most 0.03 points (2.961\%, 2.993\% and 2.971\%).
For Yq12 and its components, markers can be sampled from the published blocks or from our HMM annotation. Pooling predictions, sampling from the published blocks raised the DYZ18S median error to 4.45\% from 3.86\% and its 90th percentile to 12.43\% from 10.20\%, so Yq12 markers come from our annotation. The DYZ2 G+C floor (14 of 31 bases) was chosen on ten males sequenced by both SGDP and 1000 Genomes, where it gives a median DYZ2 difference of 4.62\% between the two libraries of each male. Two candidate Yq12 targets were dropped because of their errors (13.11\% for YqHet2.7 and 5.63\% for DYZ18sub).

\subsection{Library effects}
These errors apply to polymerase chain reaction (PCR)-free 150-bp libraries of the 1000 Genomes high-coverage project. In ten males sequenced by both SGDP (PCR-free, 100-bp reads) and 1000 Genomes, DYZ19 was 9.79\% higher in SGDP in all ten, and 4.71\% higher after cutting the 1000 Genomes reads to 100 bases. In four males sequenced PCR-free by both HGDP and SGDP, DYZ2 was 21.1\% higher in SGDP (16.9--24.8\%), Yq12 8.5\% higher and DYZ3 8.7\% lower, so lengths are compared within cohorts only. In 13 HGDP populations, PCR libraries gave DYZ2 45.5\% lower and DYZ18S 24.7\% higher than PCR-free libraries. Population analyses therefore use PCR-free libraries only.

HGDP libraries were labelled PCR-free when their CRAM file had at least two read groups. This rule matched the published library type \cite{Bergstrom2020} for 533 of the 535 HGDP files with one (397 PCR-free, 136 PCR) and labelled the other two, which are PCR-free, as PCR. SGDP libraries were labelled PCR-free when they came from LP6005 plates, and ENA records all 167 of these with a matching record as PCR-free.

\subsection{Comparison with depth-based estimates}\label{sec:m-sanroman}
Earlier short-read studies measured DYZ3 in 366 males \cite{Miga2014} and DYZ1 in 396 \cite{Altemose2014}, and used DYZ1 read depth as a proxy for Yq12 length in 1,225 males \cite{SanRoman2024}. These estimates could not be scaled or tested against complete arrays from many individuals.

San Roman et al.\ \cite{SanRoman2024} used read depth in a DYZ1 region (chrY:56,676,260--56,771,430) as a proxy for Yq12 length in relative units. They used bowtie2 alignments to hg38, corrected depth for G+C content as in Teitz et al.\ \cite{Teitz2018} and normalized it by depth in chrY:14.5--15.5 Mb. We ran their scripts (\texttt{Get\_DYZ1\_Depth.R}, commit 4dd8a71, and \texttt{calculate\_gc\_curve.py}, \texttt{unbias\_gc.py} and \texttt{find\_unique\_regions.py}, commit 9b23bce) on the 1000 Genomes 30x PCR-free alignments of the Burrows--Wheeler Aligner (BWA-MEM) of the 126 males with at least one assembled array. We adapted the scripts for CRAM input, input paths and parallel execution. We also adjusted the read count that scales the G+C curve, skipped reads whose mate could not be read, and enabled the third step of the mappability script as written.

The comparison used 83 males with an assembled DYZ1 array and Yruler cross-validation estimates (82 for Yq12 and DYZ2). For each split, we fitted a scale factor on the training males by dividing their summed assembled lengths by their summed depths. We applied this factor to the test males in 10 repeats of 5-fold cross-validation grouped by family, as for Yruler, and compared the two methods on the same males. Errors are absolute percentages of the assembled length \cite{Hallast2026}, and P values are from two-sided paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on per-male mean absolute errors.

For DYZ1 in 83 males from 82 families, mean, median, 90th percentile and maximum per-male mean errors were 2.47\%, 1.65\%, 5.38\% and 8.88\% for the depth method and 2.59\%, 2.27\%, 5.16\% and 9.91\% for Yruler (P = 0.70; smaller per-male error in 41 and 42 males). The per-male mean signed errors were correlated between methods (Spearman $\rho$ = 0.94). The coefficient of variation of assembled length divided by signal was 3.17\% for the depth method and 3.20\% for Yruler. These coefficients do not depend on the scale factor.

As a proxy for Yq12 in 82 males from 81 families, DYZ1 depth had mean, median, 90th percentile and maximum per-male mean errors of 5.52\%, 4.15\%, 12.13\% and 22.79\%, against 2.03\%, 1.71\%, 4.12\% and 7.73\% for Yruler (P = $1.0\times10^{-8}$). The corresponding coefficients of variation were 7.65\% and 2.53\%.

We also tested DYZ1 depth as a proxy for DYZ2 length. Mean, median, 90th percentile and maximum per-male mean errors were 13.47\%, 9.99\%, 31.99\% and 74.69\%, against 2.93\%, 2.34\%, 6.34\% and 13.56\% for Yruler (P = $9.6\times10^{-13}$).

To test whether sequence divergence in a lineage lowers the marker counts, we compared the estimates with aligned read depth, which tolerates mismatches and uses no Yruler marker. Reads per base in the GRCh38 DYZ3 block (chrY:10,083,500--10,621,500) and in the DYZ19 core (chrY:20,054,500--20,351,000) were divided by reads per base in chrY:14.5--15.5 Mb (Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:markerfree}a,b). Aligned depth also depends on the aligner. On the same reads of 130 males, DRAGEN 3.7.6 gave lower depth than BWA (median DYZ17 $-$30.80\%, DYZ18S $-$23.10\%, DYZ1 $-$12.80\%, Yq12 $-$12.53\%, DYZ1:DYZ2 depth ratio $-$9.85\%, DYZ19 $-$5.23\%, DYZ2 $-$3.46\%, DYZ3 $-$0.37\%), whereas Yruler counts were identical (Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:markerfree}c).

\subsection{Runtime and memory}\label{sec:m-cost}
For Fig.~\ref{fig:method}d, the released model was run on the 130 local 1000 Genomes CRAM files of males with reference assemblies (median 16.2 GB, median 31-mer depth 29.1$\times$) with 8 threads on 16-core cluster nodes, reading each CRAM directly, and timed with GNU time. Median wall-clock time was 106 s (10th--90th percentile 95--130 s, maximum 231 s), median CPU time 1,022 s and median peak memory 3.77 GB (3.76--3.79 GB). On three of these files, 1, 4, 8 and 16 threads took a median of 720, 205, 100 and 76 s with 2.92, 3.29, 3.78 and 4.74 GB. With 8 threads, the same files thinned to 15.0, 7.5, 3.0, 1.5 and 0.75$\times$ took 134, 81, 38, 23 and 12 s.

\subsection{Nanopore reads}\label{sec:m-ont}
We evaluated a separate nanopore model using ten repeats of family-grouped 5-fold cross-validation on HPRC R10.4.1 reads (dorado super-accuracy base calls) \cite{HPRCRelease2} from 82 males with assembled arrays. Markers, thresholds and scale factors were selected within each training partition. Medians of per-male mean absolute percentage errors ranged from 4.6\% to 9.7\% across the seven targets (Table~\ref{tab:ont}). The final nanopore model was then fitted using all 82 males. The reference assemblies were built from PacBio HiFi and ultra-long nanopore reads of the same individuals \cite{Hallast2026,HPRCRelease2}, so these errors may be optimistic.

The two models are trained separately and use largely overlapping marker sets (4,082 of 4,171 nanopore DYZ3 markers and 62,431 of 62,434 DYZ19 markers also occur in the short-read model). The nanopore marker and model files are in the \texttt{data/ont} folder of the Yruler repository.

We compared estimates from the final nanopore and short-read models in 82 males from the nanopore training panel (Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:markerfree}d). Absolute relative differences were calculated as $100|L_{\mathrm{ONT}}/L_{\mathrm{short}}-1|$. For DYZ3, Spearman $\rho$ was 0.972 and the median absolute relative difference was 6.8\%. Nanopore DYZ3 medians were 328 kb in R1b (7 males) and 883 kb in other lineages (75 males). The two O2a2b1a1 males had DYZ3 arrays of 262 and 286 kb, compared with 746 kb in the single O2a2b1a2 male. DYZ19 arrays were 68 and 76 kb in the two J2a1a1 males, compared with 240--310 kb in four other J males. This comparison measures agreement between platforms in the training panel.

\subsection{Cohorts, lineages and tree}\label{sec:m-population}
We applied the released model to 1,639 1000 Genomes high-coverage samples \cite{ByrskaBishop2022}: 1,597 of 1,598 males had estimates and entered population summaries. Replication used 526 PCR-free HGDP and SGDP males \cite{Bergstrom2020,Mallick2016}, including 24 SGDP males without recorded sex that were XY by X and Y dosage. Recorded sex is that of the corrected 1000 Genomes pedigree \cite{ByrskaBishop2022} and of the HGDP and SGDP sample records in ENA, and the genetic sex assigned by SGDP \cite{Mallick2016} agreed with our Y dosage in all 172 assigned SGDP samples. Lineages are HaploGrouper calls of the International Society of Genetic Genealogy (ISOGG) 2019 tree from chrY genotypes, as for 1000 Genomes. These calls agreed with the published lineages \cite{Hallast2021} in all 662 HGDP and SGDP males with both. 1000 Genomes haplogroups are HaploGrouper ISOGG 2019 calls. Haplogroups describe paternal lineages, not genome-wide ancestry. Superpopulation labels in Fig.~\ref{fig:biology}a are the 1000 Genomes metadata. Cohort locations are shown in Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:world_map}, which leaves out seven SGDP records reported as female and one SGDP record without recorded sex and without Y signal.

The tree of Fig.~\ref{fig:biology}a follows the procedure of Hallast et al.\ \cite{Hallast2026}. Variants were called with BCFtools 1.16 inside the mask of Y regions accessible to short reads \cite{Poznik2013}. Calls with depth below 3 or allele fraction below 85\% were set to missing, and sites with more than 5\% missing calls removed. RAxML 8.2.10 built one maximum-likelihood tree (GTRGAMMA, 100 rapid bootstraps) of the 1,597 males, rooted between the three A0 males and the rest. Clades were collapsed for display, and no topology or order used satellite lengths. For Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:J_DYZ19}, clades were collapsed by the first seven characters of the ISOGG 2019 call.

\subsection{Statistics}\label{sec:m-stats}
Population summaries are medians and interquartile ranges per lineage. Lineage differences were tested with Kruskal--Wallis tests across the 14 lineages with at least 20 males, in 1,248 of 1,268 males (one per kinship group). Post hoc comparisons used tie-corrected Dunn tests with Holm correction over the 91 lineage pairs of each target. These identified differences in 51 lineage pairs for DYZ3, 58 for DYZ19, 38 for Yq12 and 25 for the unit ratio (P $<$ 0.05). The share of variance accounted for by lineage is one minus the within-lineage sum of squares of length divided by its total sum of squares, in the same 1,248 males. Two-group contrasts (R1b against other lineages and O2a2b1a1 against O2a2b1a2 in DYZ3, J2a1a1 against other J in DYZ19) used two-sided Mann--Whitney tests in each cohort, with one male per kinship group in the 1000 Genomes Project. Repeating the main tests with one randomly chosen male per kinship group, 1,000 times, left every P value below 0.05 in every draw (Y dosage by lineage: P $1.4\times10^{-6}$ to 0.023). HGDP, SGDP and 1000 Genomes are always analysed separately.

In the 1000 Genomes Project, kinship groups join males connected directly or through other males by first- or second-degree KING-robust kinship \cite{Manichaikul2010} in the PLINK 2 resource \cite{Chang2015} or by kinship above 0.05 in the gnomAD relatedness table \cite{Koenig2024}. We kept one male per group, the first by sample name. In HGDP and SGDP, groups join males connected by kinship above 0.05 in the same gnomAD table, which covers 386 HGDP and 71 SGDP males of our data. KING on the SGDP genotypes of 77 further males found no pair above this value, and 3 SGDP males had no kinship data. In HGDP and SGDP, keeping one male per kinship group (527 of 537 males) changed the DYZ3 medians of R1b and of other lineages by at most 0.40\% and the SD ratios of Fig.~\ref{fig:biology}f by at most 0.004. The reported HGDP and SGDP P values use all qualifying males. Kinship filtering left the six HGDP and SGDP tests at the same order of magnitude. For R1b DYZ3, the P values were $4.4\times10^{-18}$ and $3.1\times10^{-5}$ before filtering and $5.5\times10^{-18}$ and $3.1\times10^{-5}$ after. For J2a1a1 DYZ19, they were $4\times10^{-8}$ and $5.2\times10^{-5}$ before and $5\times10^{-8}$ and $5.2\times10^{-5}$ after. For O2a2b1a1 DYZ3, they were unchanged at $2.5\times10^{-4}$ and 0.057.

The 95\% interval of the haplogroup E unit ratio comes from resampling kinship groups. For Fig.~\ref{fig:biology}f, log DYZ1 and log DYZ2 lengths were centred within broad lineages in 1,194 males without a reference assembly, one per kinship group. The ratio of their standard deviations has a bootstrap 95\% interval and was tested with the Pitman--Morgan test. To assess the contribution of DYZ2 variation to the association between Yq12 length and DYZ1 fraction, we fitted an ordinary least-squares slope with both quantities centred within lineages, in the same 1,194 males. We permuted DYZ2 lengths among males of each lineage 10,000 times, keeping DYZ1 and the rest of Yq12 fixed, and recalculated Yq12 length and DYZ1 fraction for each permutation. The observed slope was $-$1.83 percentage points per 10 Mb (95\% bootstrap interval $-$2.20 to $-$1.43; 2,000 within-lineage resamples), compared with a median of $-$0.83 (central 95\% permutation interval $-$1.01 to $-$0.64).

Phylogenetic generalized least squares on the 1000 Genomes single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) tree, with Pagel's $\lambda$ fitted by maximum likelihood, gave a slope of $-$0.287 (95\% interval $-$0.316 to $-$0.257, $\lambda$ 0.974) in 1,268 males, one per kinship group, and $-$0.332 ($-$0.442 to $-$0.222, $\lambda$ 1.000) in 83 assemblies.


Two-sided Mann--Whitney P values for R1b against other lineages in DYZ3 were $1.3\times10^{-104}$, $4.4\times10^{-18}$ and $3.1\times10^{-5}$ in 1kGP (one male per kinship group), HGDP and SGDP, respectively. For O2a2b1a1 against O2a2b1a2, they were $3.4\times10^{-9}$, $2.5\times10^{-4}$ and 0.057, the smallest value possible with 3 and 4 SGDP males. For J2a1a1 against other J in DYZ19, they were $6.7\times10^{-14}$, $4.0\times10^{-8}$ and $5.2\times10^{-5}$. Holm correction over these three contrasts left all three 1000 Genomes P values below $10^{-8}$. Unit ratios differed between lineages (Kruskal--Wallis H 238.9, P $1.3\times10^{-43}$).

Reference-array individuals in Fig.~\ref{fig:biology}a number 119 for DYZ3, 83 for Yq12 and the ratio, and 129 for DYZ19, with 119 in b, 14 in c and 8 in d. The assembly medians in e use 16 E, 13 O and 12 R1b arrays.

All P values are two-sided (Mann--Whitney, Dunn, Spearman and the Pitman--Morgan test, computed as the correlation between the sum and the difference of log DYZ1 and log DYZ2), and the Kruskal--Wallis test has no direction. No statistical method was used to predetermine sample sizes: every male with reads and the required references or estimates was included. Cross-validation folds assign family groups at random, with fixed seeds. The study analysed public data and used no blinding.
