
\subsection{Reference arrays}\label{sec:m-truth}
Reference arrays came from the 142-individual Y pangenome of Hallast et al.\ \cite{Hallast2026}. For 17 incomplete arrays (9 DYZ3, 8 Yq12) from 16 individuals, we used the HPRC release 2 assembly of the same individual \cite{Lucas2026}. Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:array-structure} shows 156 assemblies, 140 of the Y pangenome and these 16. An array was eligible when it lay on one contig with at least 100 kb of flanking sequence on both sides, contained no assembly gap and had no 1-kb bin flagged by all four quality tracks (Flagger and NucFlag, each with HiFi and nanopore reads). In one male (HG01261), a 1-kb bin inside DYZ2 was flagged by all four tracks, so its DYZ2 and Yq12 arrays were excluded and its DYZ1 and DYZ18S arrays kept.

The panels contain 129 DYZ3, 90 Yq12, 91 DYZ1, 90 DYZ2, 91 DYZ18S, 139 DYZ19 and 140 DYZ17 arrays (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:array-structure}). Of these individuals, 101, 80, 81, 80, 81, 115 and 115, respectively, have high-coverage 1000 Genomes short reads \cite{ByrskaBishop2022} and form the evaluation sets.

Reference coverage is uneven across the tree. Of the 33 lineages of Fig.~\ref{fig:biology}a, 5 have no evaluated DYZ3 reference (J2a1*, J2a1a2, J2a2, O2a2b2 and O2b, 10 males). Seven have none for Yq12 (those five plus NO* and O1a, 31 males). Their estimates therefore rest on the excluded-lineage performance.

\subsection{Satellite annotation}\label{sec:m-units}
Reference lengths follow the annotation of Hallast et al.\ \cite{Hallast2026}. DYZ3 and Yq12 use the array coordinates, and DYZ19 uses the main-array span from their script \texttt{filterDYZ19HMMER.py}. DYZ1, DYZ2 and DYZ18S (DYZ18 plus the 3.1-kb repeat) are sums of the HMMER blocks retained by their motifs and script \texttt{filterYq12HMMER.py} (\url{https://github.com/Markloftus/PanYChromosome}) \cite{Hallast2026}. Assemblies without a published annotation, including the HPRC release 2 replacements, were annotated with the same motifs, nhmmer options and filters \cite{Hallast2026,Wheeler2013}. DYZ17 is not in that annotation: we built a profile HMM from start-to-start repeat units of 140 assemblies with MAFFT \cite{Katoh2013}, hmmbuild and hmmemit \cite{Eddy2011} (5,573-bp consensus). Hits less than 10 kb apart were joined into blocks. DYZ17 length was the span of the longest block, from its first to its last hit. Our own profile-HMM annotation of Yq12 defines only the regions from which markers are sampled.

\subsection{Marker selection}\label{sec:m-markers}
Canonical 31-mers were counted in the reference arrays with KMC 3.2.4 \cite{Kokot2017}. Candidates had to occur in at least 15\% of the assemblies of a class and nowhere in the non-Y sequence of CHM13 \cite{Nurk2022} or of 71 high-quality HPRC release 2 haplotypes from individuals outside the study. A marker of class $c$ had to occur in at least a fraction $p$ of the class assemblies and outside the class in fewer than a fraction $o$ of the Y assemblies. We chose $p\in\{0.50,0.60,0.70,0.80,0.90,0.95\}$ and $o\in\{0,0.05,0.10,0.25\}$ for each class by 5-fold cross-validation inside each training partition. Here $o=0$ requires absence outside the class.

The single-copy Y ruler contains X-degenerate 31-mers present in at least 90\% of the training assemblies. In 149 assemblies, a median of 99.49\% of ruler 31-mers occurred once on the Y.

The released model contains 6,183 DYZ3, 201,987 DYZ1, 30,442 DYZ2, 28,650 DYZ18S, 91,086 DYZ19, 75,149 DYZ17 and 21,417 Yq12-residual markers, 6,860,192 ruler markers and 386,153 markers of an autosomal window at chr8:91--101 Mb (Autosomal window). It also counts 577,108 markers of the whole Yq12, which are not used for the estimate. Masked superstrings \cite{Sladky2023} store the marker set.

\subsection{Autosomal window}\label{sec:m-window}
Y dosage needs an autosomal reference with a stable copy number. We tested 2,503 autosomal windows of GRCh38. We aligned each of the 465 HPRC release 2 haplotypes to GRCh38 with minimap2 2.31 (\texttt{-x asm20 -{}-eqx -c}) \cite{Li2018}. We built an implicit pangenome graph from these alignments with IMPG 0.5.0 \cite{IMPG} to project each window into the haplotypes. We ranked the windows with equal weight on six criteria: conservation and single-copy state across the haplotypes, each in the worst superpopulation, copy-number variation in the reference males, difference in median copy number between superpopulations, agreement of Y dosage between libraries of 17 males sequenced more than once, and G+C content close to that of the ruler. We used chr8:91--101 Mb, the highest-ranked window whose dose stayed within 1.89\% of the median of five windows (chr8:91--101, chr11:26--36, chr2:198--208, chr1:87--97 and chr6:44--54 Mb) in every sample. Its markers are 31-mers of its projections into 101 high-quality haplotypes, present in at least 90\% of these haplotypes and at one copy in at least 95\% of the HPRC haplotypes of every superpopulation (386,153 of 450,821).

\subsection{Read counting}\label{sec:m-counting}
Yruler reads BAM, CRAM, SAM, FASTQ or FASTA files through HTSlib \cite{Bonfield2021}, including unmapped reads. Indexed BAM and CRAM files are read in parallel. Secondary and supplementary records are skipped. Duplicate reads are retained, and no mapping-quality or base-quality filter is applied. In the 1,587 males of the population analyses, the duplicate fraction (median 9.04\%, 10th--90th percentile 7.09--11.12\%) did not correlate with any satellite length within lineages ($|\rho|\le0.073$). Removing duplicate reads in 30 males reduced DYZ2 estimates by a median of 16.18\% and increased DYZ3 estimates by 7.89\%. Every canonical 31-mer occurrence adds one to its marker count. Yruler writes per-marker counts, class sums and estimates.

\subsection{G+C correction}\label{sec:m-gc}
Libraries differ in their recovery of reads poor in G and C, which affects array estimates. Yruler derives a correction from the ruler markers in each sample. Counts above $10\times(\mathrm{mean}+1)$, where the mean is over all ruler markers, are omitted from the correction curve. For each 31-mer G+C content, relative depth is the mean retained ruler count at that content divided by the overall mean retained ruler count. Correction requires at least 1,000,000 ruler counts. A G+C bin needs at least 1,000 ruler markers and 10,000 retained counts, and other bins take the nearest qualifying value. Each class marker count is divided by the relative depth at its G+C content. Class sums are then multiplied by the mean relative depth over all ruler markers to keep class and ruler counts on the same scale. Without a qualifying curve, raw counts are used.

DYZ2 is rich in A and T, and its markers with few G or C bases gave the largest differences between libraries of the same male. DYZ2 markers therefore require at least 14 G or C bases of 31, a threshold chosen on ten males sequenced by both the Simons Genome Diversity Project (SGDP) and 1000 Genomes, without assembly lengths.

\subsection{Length estimation}\label{sec:m-length}
For class $c$ in individual $i$, the corrected marker count $S^{\mathrm{GC}}_{ic}$ is divided by $S_{iY}$, the sum of the single-copy Y marker counts. A class-specific factor $K_c$, calculated as the sum of training array lengths divided by the sum of their count ratios, converts the ratio into base pairs,
\begin{equation}
 \widehat L_{ic}=K_c\,\frac{S^{\mathrm{GC}}_{ic}}{S_{iY}}.
\end{equation}
Yq12 length is the sum of DYZ1, DYZ2, DYZ18S and the rest of Yq12 (other repeats and unannotated sequence), which has its own markers and $K$. On the same 80 males and folds, this sum gave a median Yq12 error of 1.70\%, against 1.75\% for a single Yq12 marker class.
The DYZ1:DYZ2 length ratio is converted into a ratio of repeat units with the median annotated unit lengths, 3,478.9 bp for DYZ1 and 2,404.4 bp for DYZ2 \cite{Hallast2026}.

\subsection{Y dosage and Y state}\label{sec:m-dosage}
Y dosage is
\begin{equation}
 D_i=\frac{2}{s}\,\frac{S_{iY}/n_Y}{S_{iA}/n_A},
\end{equation}
where $S_{iA}$ is the count of the chr8:91--101 Mb window and $n_Y$ and $n_A$ are the numbers of markers. The scale factor $s=0.9614$ is the median unscaled dosage $2(S_{iY}/n_Y)/(S_{iA}/n_A)$ among 1000 Genomes samples with dosage above half the cohort median.

Yruler reports no detectable Y signal for $D_i\le0.02$. This threshold exceeds the highest value in 346 females (208 from the 1000 Genomes Project, 44 from the Human Genome Diversity Project (HGDP) and 94 from SGDP, 0.0191). For these samples, satellite outputs report background at haploid autosomal depth. Because the array and the ruler scale together, a uniform loss of Y (mosaic loss) leaves the estimated length per Y unchanged.

For screening, the ruler markers were also summed separately over Yp (X-degenerate blocks 1--2, 850,505 markers) and proximal Yq (blocks 3--8, 6,000,853 markers). We omitted 8,834 ruler markers without a block shared by at least 90\% of the assemblies. We also counted 31-mers unique to CHM13v2 chrY in 100-kb windows. Both profiles were divided by the chr8 window and by the median of males without flagged Y profiles (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:chrY-unusual}). X, PAR1 and PAR2 doses use 31-mers of the HPRC haplotypes in the same way.

Samples were flagged, in this order, for Yp depletion (Yp below 0.5, Yq within 0.1 of one), Yq loss (Yp within 0.1 of one and Yq more than 0.1 below Yp) and arm imbalance (Yp and Yq more than 0.1 apart). Subsequent categories were PAR2 gain (PAR2 dose at least 1.25 with PAR1 within 0.1 of one), Y gain (dosage at least 1.75), intermediate gain (1.25 to 1.75) or low dosage (below 0.9). These flags are screening categories, not confirmed karyotypes. Four arm-imbalanced or Yq-loss and six Yp-depleted males were excluded from population summaries, and the four males with a PAR2 gain from the Yq12, DYZ1, DYZ2 and ratio summaries. The per-male table marks estimates outside the range of the reference arrays (129 of 1,587 males in at least one class).

To test performance at low Y abundance, we mixed reads from 135 male--female pairs at fixed proportions. The median DYZ3 error was 0.36\% at a Y fraction of 0.5, 3.16\% at 0.10 and 6.47\% at 0.05, and the median Yq12 error 0.38\%, 3.16\% and 6.07\%.

\subsection{Accuracy evaluation}\label{sec:m-benchmark}
Accuracy was measured with ten repeats of 5-fold cross-validation, with individuals of the same recorded family kept in the same fold. Markers, thresholds and $K_c$ were fitted on the four training folds only. Array lengths were predicted for individuals in the held-out fold. The error is $100|\widehat L_i/L_i-1|$ (Table~\ref{tab:accuracy}).

In a second test, each of 18 broad Y lineages was excluded in turn from training, together with any family shared with test individuals, and predicted from the remaining references (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:lineage-validation}). For depth, test reads were thinned to 20, 15, 10, 5, 2 and 1$\times$ base coverage while training reads kept full depth (Fig.~\ref{fig:method}e, and Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:y-dosage} for Y dosage).

Parent--offspring concordance used the 309 pairs of the recorded 1000 Genomes pedigree among males with estimates. Of these, 282 pairs have the parent in the original 2,504-sample release and the offspring in the 698-sample extension, so they also compare sequencing batches (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:all-pedigrees}). Y specificity used 243 female genomes held out from marker selection, with the ruler replaced by its expected sum at the female haploid depth.

Twenty-one further males with reads and reference arrays were held out from all model choices. Their median errors were 1.52\% for DYZ3 (18 males), 1.02\% for DYZ17 (15), 2.90\% for DYZ19 (14), and 0.52\% for Yq12, 0.57\% for DYZ1, 2.34\% for DYZ2 and 3.41\% for DYZ18S (3 males each).

Short DYZ19 arrays span 62.3--63.5 kb in four J2a reference chromosomes, against a median reference length of 321.7 kb. Mean absolute percentage errors across the ten cross-validation repeats were 3.12\%, 3.79\% and 11.34\% in the three J2a1a1 males, and the error was 3.31\% in a fourth, held-out male (Fig.~\ref{fig:method}c). DYZ18S shares 10,384 of its 28,650 31-mers with DYZ1 and had a median cross-validation error of 3.92\% (Table~\ref{tab:accuracy}).

\subsection{Model choices}
For Yq12 and its components, markers can be sampled from the published blocks or from our HMM annotation. Sampling from the published blocks raised the DYZ18S median error to 4.48\% from 3.92\% and its 90th percentile to 13.90\% from 10.41\%, so Yq12 markers come from our annotation. The DYZ2 G+C floor was chosen on ten males sequenced by both SGDP and 1000 Genomes. Two further Yq12 classes were dropped because of their errors (13.11\% for YqHet2.7 and 5.63\% for DYZ18sub).

\subsection{Library effects}
These errors apply to PCR-free 150-bp libraries of the 1000 Genomes high-coverage project. In ten males sequenced by both SGDP (PCR-free, 100-bp reads) and 1000 Genomes, DYZ19 was 9.80\% higher in SGDP in all ten, and 4.71\% higher after cutting the 1000 Genomes reads to 100 bases. In four males sequenced PCR-free by both HGDP and SGDP, DYZ2 was 21.1\% higher in SGDP (16.9--24.8\%), Yq12 8.5\% higher and DYZ3 8.7\% lower, so lengths are compared within cohorts only. In 13 HGDP populations, PCR libraries gave DYZ2 45.5\% lower and DYZ18S 24.7\% higher than PCR-free libraries. Population analyses therefore use PCR-free libraries only.

HGDP libraries were labelled PCR-free when their CRAM file had at least two read groups. This rule matched the published library type \cite{Bergstrom2020} for 533 of the 535 HGDP files with one (397 PCR-free, 136 PCR) and labelled the other two, which are PCR-free, as PCR. SGDP libraries were labelled PCR-free when they came from LP6005 plates, and ENA records all 167 of these with a matching record as PCR-free.

\subsection{Comparison with depth-based estimates}\label{sec:m-sanroman}
Earlier short-read studies measured DYZ3 in 366 males \cite{Miga2014} and DYZ1 in 396 \cite{Altemose2014}, and used DYZ1 read depth as a proxy for Yq12 length in 1,225 males \cite{SanRoman2024}. These estimates could not be calibrated or tested against complete arrays from many individuals.

San Roman et al.\ \cite{SanRoman2024} estimated Yq12 length from read depth in a DYZ1 region of 1000 Genomes Phase 3 alignments. We took their published values (Table S6A) for the 30 males with assembled arrays and ran Yruler on the same Phase 3 runs, whose libraries were PCR-amplified. Two comparisons do not depend on a scale factor: the Spearman correlation of each estimate with assembled length, and the coefficient of variation of assembled length divided by the estimate across males. For base-pair errors, Yruler used the released model, and the depth values were scaled with one factor fitted on these 30 males. All 30 are also Yruler calibration males, so both comparisons are in-sample. For DYZ1 in our same-data comparison, Spearman correlations were 0.54 for Yruler and 0.47 for the depth estimates, with coefficients of variation of assembled length divided by estimate of 23.6\% and 25.2\%, respectively. Over 4,000 resamples of the 30 males, Yruler minus San Roman Spearman correlation was 0.40 (95\% interval 0.17--0.68) for Yq12 and 0.06 ($-$0.03 to 0.17) for DYZ1, and the San Roman coefficient of variation divided by that of Yruler was 1.70 (1.46--2.17) and 1.07 (0.98--1.19).

To test whether sequence divergence in a lineage lowers the marker counts, we compared the estimates with aligned read depth, which tolerates mismatches and uses no Yruler marker. Reads per base in the GRCh38 DYZ3 block (chrY:10,083,500--10,621,500) and in the DYZ19 core (chrY:20,054,500--20,351,000) were divided by reads per base in chrY:14.5--15.5 Mb (Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:markerfree}). Aligned depth also depends on the aligner. On the same reads of 130 males, DRAGEN 3.7.6 gave lower depth than BWA (median DYZ17 $-$30.80\%, DYZ18S $-$23.10\%, DYZ1 $-$12.80\%, Yq12 $-$12.53\%, DYZ1:DYZ2 depth ratio $-$9.85\%, DYZ19 $-$5.23\%, DYZ2 $-$3.46\%, DYZ3 $-$0.37\%), whereas Yruler counts were identical (Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:markerfree}c).

\subsection{Runtime and memory}\label{sec:m-cost}
For Fig.~\ref{fig:method}d, the released model was run on the 130 local 1000 Genomes CRAM files of males with reference assemblies (median 16.2 GB, median 31-mer depth 29.1$\times$) with 8 threads on 16-core cluster nodes, reading each CRAM directly, and timed with GNU time. Median wall-clock time was 86 s (10th--90th percentile 80--97 s, maximum 127 s), median CPU time 930 s and median peak memory 3.48 GB (3.46--3.50 GB). On three of these files, 1, 4, 8 and 16 threads took a median of 600, 159, 82 and 61 s with 2.61, 3.00, 3.46 and 4.46 GB. With 8 threads, the same files thinned to 15.0, 7.5, 3.0, 1.5 and 0.75$\times$ took 58, 32, 16, 9.0 and 6.4 s.

\subsection{Nanopore reads}\label{sec:m-ont}
The same procedure, with markers, thresholds and scale factors learned from nanopore reads within the training folds, was applied to HPRC R10.4.1 reads (dorado super-accuracy base calls) \cite{HPRCRelease2} of 84 males with assembled arrays. Median errors were 1.9 to 3.6 times those of short reads (Table~\ref{tab:ont}).

\subsection{Cohorts, lineages and tree}\label{sec:m-population}
We applied the released model to 1,639 1000 Genomes high-coverage samples \cite{ByrskaBishop2022}: 1,597 of 1,598 males had estimates, and 1,587 entered population summaries. Replication used 526 PCR-free HGDP and SGDP males \cite{Bergstrom2020,Mallick2016}, including 24 SGDP males without recorded sex that were XY by X and Y dosage, with HaploGrouper ISOGG 2019 calls from chrY genotypes, as for 1000 Genomes. These calls agreed with the published lineages \cite{Hallast2021} in all 662 HGDP and SGDP males with both. 1000 Genomes haplogroups are HaploGrouper ISOGG 2019 calls. Haplogroups describe paternal lineages, not genome-wide ancestry. Superpopulation labels in Fig.~\ref{fig:biology}a are the 1000 Genomes metadata. Cohort locations are shown in Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:map}, which leaves out seven SGDP records reported as female and one SGDP record without recorded sex and without Y signal.

The tree of Fig.~\ref{fig:biology}a follows the procedure of Hallast et al.\ \cite{Hallast2026}. Variants were called with BCFtools 1.16 inside the mask of Y regions accessible to short reads \cite{Poznik2013}. Calls with depth below 3 or allele fraction below 85\% were set to missing, and sites with more than 5\% missing calls removed. RAxML 8.2.10 built one maximum-likelihood tree (GTRGAMMA, 100 rapid bootstraps) of the 1,597 males, rooted between the three A0 males and the rest. Clades were collapsed for display, and no topology or order used satellite lengths. For Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:j-dyz19}, clades were collapsed by the first seven characters of the ISOGG 2019 call.

\subsection{Statistics}\label{sec:m-stats}
Population summaries are medians and interquartile ranges per lineage. Lineage differences were tested with Kruskal--Wallis tests across the 14 lineages with at least 20 males, in 1,250 of 1,271 males (one per kinship component, PAR2 gains removed). Post hoc comparisons used tie-corrected Dunn tests with Holm correction over the 91 lineage pairs of each target. These identified differences in 51 lineage pairs for DYZ3, 59 for DYZ19, 39 for Yq12 and 25 for the unit ratio (P $<$ 0.05). The share of variance explained by lineage is one minus the within-lineage sum of squares of log length divided by its total sum of squares, in the same 1,250 males. Two-group contrasts (R1b against other lineages and O2a2b1a1 against O2a2b1a2 in DYZ3, J2a1a1 against other J in DYZ19) used two-sided Mann--Whitney tests in each cohort, with one male per kinship component in the 1000 Genomes Project. HGDP, SGDP and 1000 Genomes are always analysed separately.

In the 1000 Genomes Project, kinship components join males with a first- or second-degree KING-robust kinship \cite{Manichaikul2010} in the PLINK 2 resource \cite{Chang2015}, and one male per component, the first by sample name, was kept. In HGDP and SGDP, components join males with a kinship of at least 0.0442 (third degree) in the gnomAD relatedness table \cite{Koenig2024}, which covers 386 HGDP and 62 SGDP males. KING on the SGDP genotypes of 75 further males found no pair at or above this value, and 3 SGDP males had no kinship data. In HGDP and SGDP, keeping one male per kinship component (516 of 526 males) changed the DYZ3 medians of R1b and of other lineages by 0.36\% and the SD ratios of Fig.~\ref{fig:biology}f by at most 0.004. The reported HGDP and SGDP P values use all qualifying males. Kinship filtering left the six HGDP and SGDP tests at the same order of magnitude. For R1b DYZ3, the P values were $4\times10^{-18}$ and $1.8\times10^{-5}$ before filtering and $5\times10^{-18}$ and $1.9\times10^{-5}$ after. For J2a1a1 DYZ19, they were $4\times10^{-8}$ and $7.4\times10^{-5}$ before and $5\times10^{-8}$ and $7.4\times10^{-5}$ after. For O2a2b1a1 DYZ3, they were unchanged at $2.5\times10^{-4}$ and 0.057.

The 95\% interval of the haplogroup E unit ratio comes from resampling kinship components. For Fig.~\ref{fig:biology}f, log DYZ1 and log DYZ2 lengths were centred within broad lineages in 1,196 males without a reference assembly, one per kinship component. The ratio of their standard deviations has a bootstrap 95\% interval and was tested with the Pitman--Morgan test. The arithmetic expectation of the within-lineage slope of log DYZ1:DYZ2 on log Yq12 comes from 1,000 permutations of DYZ2 among males of the same lineage.

Phylogenetic generalized least squares on the 1000 Genomes SNP tree, with Pagel's $\lambda$ fitted by maximum likelihood, gave a slope of $-$0.289 (95\% interval $-$0.319 to $-$0.259, $\lambda$ 0.972) in 1,271 males, one per kinship component, and $-$0.332 ($-$0.442 to $-$0.222, $\lambda$ 1.000) in 83 assemblies.


Two-sided Mann--Whitney P values for R1b against other lineages in DYZ3 were $1.4\times10^{-103}$, $4.0\times10^{-18}$ and $1.8\times10^{-5}$ in 1kGP (one male per kinship component), HGDP and SGDP, respectively. For O2a2b1a1 against O2a2b1a2, they were $2.7\times10^{-9}$, $2.5\times10^{-4}$ and 0.057, the smallest value possible with 3 and 4 SGDP males. For J2a1a1 against other J in DYZ19, they were $1.4\times10^{-13}$, $4.0\times10^{-8}$ and $7.4\times10^{-5}$. Unit ratios differed between lineages (Kruskal--Wallis H 247.1, P $2.6\times10^{-45}$).

Reference-array individuals in Fig.~\ref{fig:biology}a number 119 for DYZ3, 83 for Yq12 and the ratio, and 129 for DYZ19, with 119 in b, 14 in c and 8 in d. The assembly medians in e use 16 E, 13 O and 12 R1b arrays.
