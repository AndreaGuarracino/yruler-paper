
\subsection{Reference arrays}\label{sec:m-truth}
Reference arrays came from the 142-individual Y pangenome of Hallast et al.\ \cite{Hallast2026}, with HPRC release 2 assemblies \cite{Lucas2026} replacing arrays that failed completeness criteria. An array was eligible when it lay on one contig with at least 100 kb of flanking sequence on both sides, contained no assembly gap and had no 1-kb bin flagged by all four quality tracks (Flagger and NucFlag, each with HiFi and nanopore reads). The panels contain 129 DYZ3, 90 Yq12, 91 DYZ1, 90 DYZ2, 91 DYZ18S, 139 DYZ19 and 140 DYZ17 arrays (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:array-structure}). Of these individuals, 101, 80, 81, 80, 81, 115 and 115, respectively, have high-coverage 1000 Genomes short reads \cite{ByrskaBishop2022} and form the evaluation sets. Twenty-one further males with reads and reference arrays were held out from all model choices.

\subsection{Satellite annotation}\label{sec:m-units}
Reference lengths follow the annotation of Hallast et al.\ \cite{Hallast2026}. DYZ3 and Yq12 use the array coordinates, and DYZ19 uses the main-array span from their script \texttt{filterDYZ19HMMER.py}. DYZ1, DYZ2 and DYZ18S (DYZ18 plus the 3.1-kb repeat) are sums of the HMMER blocks retained by their motifs and script \texttt{filterYq12HMMER.py} (\url{https://github.com/Markloftus/PanYChromosome}) \cite{Hallast2026}. This reproduced their published DYZ1 and DYZ2 amounts in all 84 comparable assemblies. Assemblies without a published annotation, including the HPRC replacements, were annotated with the same motifs, nhmmer options and filters \cite{Hallast2026,Wheeler2013}. DYZ17 is not in that annotation: we built a profile HMM from start-to-start repeat units of 140 assemblies with MAFFT \cite{Katoh2013}, hmmbuild and hmmemit \cite{Eddy2011} (5,573-bp consensus), and took as the target the span of the longest run of hits separated by at most 10 kb. Our own profile-HMM annotation of Yq12 defines only the regions from which markers are sampled. Marker sampling used every assembly in a panel, including the Yq12 and DYZ2 regions of HG01261, whose lengths failed the four-track rule and were left out of calibration and evaluation.

\subsection{Marker selection}\label{sec:m-markers}
Canonical 31-mers were counted in the reference arrays with KMC 3.2.4 \cite{Kokot2017}. Candidates had to occur in at least 15\% of the assemblies of a class and nowhere in the non-Y sequence of CHM13 \cite{Nurk2022} or of 71 HPRC release 2 haplotypes from individuals outside the study. A marker of class $c$ had to occur in at least a fraction $p$ of the class assemblies and outside the class in fewer than a fraction $o$ of the Y assemblies, with $p\in\{0.50,0.60,0.70,0.80,0.90,0.95\}$ and $o\in\{0,0.05,0.10,0.25\}$ chosen for each class by 5-fold cross-validation inside each training partition. DYZ2 markers also need at least 14 G or C bases. The single-copy Y ruler contains X-degenerate 31-mers present in at least 90\% of the training assemblies; in 149 assemblies a median of 99.49\% of ruler 31-mers occurred once on the Y. The released model contains 6,183 DYZ3, 201,987 DYZ1, 30,176 DYZ2, 28,650 DYZ18S, 91,086 DYZ19, 75,149 DYZ17 and 21,417 Yq12-residual markers, 6,860,192 ruler markers and 386,153 markers of an autosomal window at chr8:91--101 Mb. Of 2,503 candidate windows ranked for conservation, single-copy state and copy-number stability in 101 HPRC haplotypes, it was the highest-ranked window that stayed within 1.89\% of the median of five windows in every sample; the two higher-ranked windows, on chromosome 11, shifted by up to 24.7\% in three males with a chromosome 11 gain. Masked superstrings \cite{Sladky2023} store the marker set.

\subsection{Read counting}\label{sec:m-counting}
Yruler reads BAM, CRAM, SAM, FASTQ or FASTA files through HTSlib \cite{Bonfield2021}, including unmapped reads; indexed BAM and CRAM files are read in parallel 10-Mb shards plus the unmapped reads. Secondary and supplementary records are skipped. No mapping-quality, duplicate or base-quality filter is applied, and ambiguous bases interrupt k-mer generation. Every canonical 31-mer occurrence adds one to its marker count, looked up through a Bloom filter and a hash table. Yruler writes per-marker counts, class sums and estimates.

\subsection{Length estimation}\label{sec:m-length}
For individual $i$, let $C_{ik}$ be the count of marker $k$ and $g(k)$ its number of G or C bases. A relative depth curve $r_i(g)$ is the mean count of the ruler markers with $g$ G or C bases divided by the mean of all ruler markers, after removing counts above ten times the mean plus ten; G+C bins with fewer than 1,000 markers or 10,000 counts take the value of the nearest supported bin. With $f_i$ the mean of $r_i(g(k))$ over the ruler markers,
\begin{equation}
 S^{\mathrm{GC}}_{ic}=f_i\sum_{k\in M_c}\frac{C_{ik}}{r_i(g(k))},
 \qquad S_{iY}=\sum_{k\in M_Y}C_{ik},
\end{equation}
where $M_c$ are the markers of class $c$ and $M_Y$ the ruler. Samples with fewer than 1,000,000 ruler counts use uncorrected sums. The length estimate and the scale factor are
\begin{equation}
 \widehat L_{ic}=K_c z_{ic},\qquad
 z_{ic}=\frac{S^{\mathrm{GC}}_{ic}}{S_{iY}},\qquad
 K_c=\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal T_c}L_{ic}}{\sum_{i\in\mathcal T_c}z_{ic}},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal T_c$ are the training individuals with an assembled length $L_{ic}$. Yq12 is the sum of its parts,
\begin{equation}
 \widehat L_{i,\mathrm{Yq12}}=\widehat L_{i,\mathrm{DYZ1}}+\widehat L_{i,\mathrm{DYZ2}}+\widehat L_{i,\mathrm{DYZ18S}}+\widehat L_{i,\mathrm{rest}},
\end{equation}
where the rest (other repeats and unannotated sequence of Yq12) has its own markers and scale. On the same 80 males and folds, this sum gave a median Yq12 error of 1.72\%, against 1.75\% for a single Yq12 marker class. The DYZ1:DYZ2 ratio is converted into a ratio of repeat units with the median unit lengths of the annotated assemblies, 3,478.9 bp for DYZ1 and 2,404.4 bp for DYZ2 \cite{Hallast2026}.

\subsection{Y dosage and Y state}\label{sec:m-dosage}
Y dosage is
\begin{equation}
 D_i=\frac{2}{s}\,\frac{S_{iY}/n_Y}{S_{iA}/n_A},
\end{equation}
where $S_{iA}$ is the count of the chr8:91--101 Mb window, $n_Y$ and $n_A$ are the numbers of markers and $s=0.9614$ is the median unscaled ratio in 1000 Genomes samples with dosage above half the cohort median. A sample with $D_i\le0.0067$, the highest value in 208 1000 Genomes females, has no Y; its output is background at haploid autosomal depth. Because the array and the ruler scale together, a uniform loss of Y (mosaic loss) leaves the estimated length per Y unchanged. For screening, the ruler markers were also summed separately over Yp (X-degenerate blocks 1--2, 850,505 markers) and proximal Yq (blocks 3--8, 6,000,853 markers), and 31-mers that occur only on CHM13v2 chrY were counted in 100-kb windows, both divided by the chr8 window and by the median of normal males (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:chrY-unusual}). X, PAR1 and PAR2 doses use 31-mers of the HPRC haplotypes in the same way. Samples were flagged, in this order, for Yp depletion (Yp below 0.5, Yq within 0.1 of one), Yq loss, arm imbalance (Yp and Yq more than 0.1 apart), PAR2 gain (PAR2 dose at least 1.25 with PAR1 within 0.1 of one), Y gain or low dosage (below 0.9). Four arm-imbalanced or Yq-loss and six Yp-depleted males were excluded from population summaries, and the four males with a PAR2 gain from the Yq12, DYZ1, DYZ2 and ratio summaries. These flags are screening categories, not confirmed karyotypes.

\subsection{Accuracy evaluation}\label{sec:m-benchmark}
Accuracy was measured with ten repeats of 5-fold cross-validation, with individuals of the same recorded family kept in the same fold. Markers, thresholds and $K_c$ were fitted on the four training folds only, and each individual was predicted from the fifth. The error is $100|\widehat L_i/L_i-1|$ (Table~\ref{tab:accuracy}). In a second test, each of 18 broad Y lineages was excluded in turn from training, together with any family shared with test individuals, and predicted from the remaining references (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:lineage-validation}). For depth, test reads were thinned to 20, 15, 10, 5, 2 and 1$\times$ base coverage while training reads kept full depth (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:s-depth}). Parent--offspring concordance used the 309 pairs of the recorded 1000 Genomes pedigree among males with estimates; 282 pairs have the parent in the original 2,504-sample release and the offspring in the 698-sample extension, so they also compare sequencing batches (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:all-pedigrees}). Y specificity used 243 female genomes held out from marker selection, with the ruler replaced by its expected sum at the female haploid depth. Three choices used cross-validation results and are reported here: the marker-sampling variant was chosen by a rule fixed before the results (it changed DYZ18S, median error 3.93\% against 4.48\%); the DYZ2 G+C floor was chosen on ten males sequenced by both SGDP and 1000 Genomes; and two further Yq12 classes were dropped because of their errors (YqHet2.7, 13.11\%; DYZ18sub, 5.63\%). These errors apply to PCR-free 150-bp libraries of the calibration project. In ten males sequenced by both SGDP (PCR-free, 100-bp reads) and 1000 Genomes, DYZ19 was 9.80\% higher in SGDP in all ten, and 4.71\% higher after cutting the 1000 Genomes reads to 100 bases; in 13 HGDP populations, PCR libraries gave DYZ2 45.5\% lower and DYZ18S 24.7\% higher than PCR-free libraries. Population analyses therefore use PCR-free libraries only.

\subsection{Comparison with depth-based estimates}\label{sec:m-sanroman}
San Roman et al.\ \cite{SanRoman2024} estimated Yq12 length from read depth in a DYZ1 region of 1000 Genomes Phase 3 alignments. We took their published values (Table S6A) for the 30 males with assembled arrays and ran Yruler on the same Phase 3 runs, whose libraries were PCR-amplified. Two comparisons do not depend on a scale factor: the Spearman correlation of each estimate with assembled length, and the coefficient of variation of assembled length divided by the estimate across males. For base-pair errors, each method was scaled with one factor fitted on these 30 males. All 30 are also Yruler calibration males, so both comparisons are in-sample.

\subsection{Nanopore reads (preliminary)}\label{sec:m-ont}
The same procedure, with markers, thresholds and scale factors learned from nanopore reads within the training folds, was applied to HPRC R10.4.1 reads (dorado super-accuracy base calls) \cite{HPRCRelease2} of 84 males with assembled arrays. Median errors were 1.9 to 3.6 times those of short reads (Table~\ref{tab:ont}). These values are preliminary and will be updated with the released marker set.

\subsection{Cohorts, lineages and tree}\label{sec:m-population}
We applied the released model to 1,639 1000 Genomes high-coverage samples \cite{ByrskaBishop2022}: 1,597 of 1,598 males had estimates, and 1,587 entered population summaries. Replication used 518 PCR-free HGDP and SGDP males \cite{Bergstrom2020,Mallick2016} with published haplogroups \cite{Hallast2021}, completed with HaploGrouper ISOGG 2019 calls from chrY genotypes for 21 individuals. 1000 Genomes haplogroups are HaploGrouper ISOGG 2019 calls. Haplogroups describe paternal lineages, not genome-wide ancestry; superpopulation labels in Fig.~\ref{fig:biology}a are the 1000 Genomes metadata. Cohort locations are shown in Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:map}.

The tree of Fig.~\ref{fig:biology}a follows the procedure of Hallast et al.\ \cite{Hallast2026}. Variants were called with BCFtools 1.16 inside the mask of Y regions accessible to short reads \cite{Poznik2013}; calls with depth below 3 or allele fraction below 85\% were set to missing, and sites with more than 5\% missing calls removed. RAxML 8.2.10 built one maximum-likelihood tree (GTRGAMMA, 100 rapid bootstraps) of the 1,597 males, rooted between the three A0 males and the rest. Clades were collapsed for display; no topology or order used satellite lengths.

\subsection{Statistics}\label{sec:m-stats}
Population summaries are medians and interquartile ranges per lineage. Lineage differences were tested with Kruskal--Wallis tests across the 14 lineages with at least 20 males, in 1,250 of 1,271 males (one per kinship component, PAR2 gains removed), with Dunn tests and Holm correction. The 95\% interval of the haplogroup E unit ratio comes from resampling kinship components. For Fig.~\ref{fig:biology}f, log DYZ1 and log DYZ2 lengths were centred within broad lineages in 1,196 males without a reference assembly, one per kinship component; the ratio of their standard deviations has a bootstrap 95\% interval and was tested with the Pitman--Morgan test. HGDP, SGDP and 1000 Genomes are always analysed separately.
