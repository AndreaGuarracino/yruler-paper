
\subsection{Reference arrays}\label{sec:m-truth}
Reference arrays came from the Y pangenome of Hallast et al.\ \cite{Hallast2026} and HPRC release 2 assemblies \cite{Lucas2026}. For 17 arrays incomplete in the Hallast assemblies (9 DYZ3 and 8 Yq12), we used the HPRC assembly of the same individual. An array was eligible when it lay on one contig with at least 100 kb of flanking sequence on both sides, contained no assembly gap and had no 1-kb bin flagged by all four quality tracks (Flagger and NucFlag, each with HiFi and nanopore reads). When a Yq12 component failed the quality check, we excluded that component and the whole Yq12 array, but retained the other components. The reference assemblies provided 129 DYZ3, 90 Yq12, 91 DYZ1, 90 DYZ2, 91 DYZ18S, 139 DYZ19 and 140 DYZ17 measurements (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:array_structure}). Among the individuals with these reference measurements, 114, 82, 83, 82, 83, 125 and 126, respectively, have high-coverage 1kGP short reads \cite{ByrskaBishop2022} and form the evaluation sets.

Reference assemblies are unevenly distributed across the Y tree. Of the 33 lineages in Fig.~\ref{fig:biology}a, five have no evaluated reference for DYZ3 (10 males) and seven have none for Yq12 (31 males). The excluded-lineage tests assess performance on lineages absent from training (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:lineage_validation}a).

\subsection{Satellite annotation}\label{sec:m-units}
Reference lengths follow the annotation of Hallast et al.\ \cite{Hallast2026}. For DYZ3, Yq12 and DYZ19, reference lengths were measured between the annotated array boundaries. For DYZ1, DYZ2 and DYZ18S, we summed the lengths of the blocks annotated for each repeat. For DYZ18S, we combined DYZ18 with another Yq12 repeat of approximately 3.1 kb \cite{Hallast2023}. Assemblies without a published annotation, including the HPRC release 2 replacements, were annotated with the same motifs, nhmmer \cite{Wheeler2013} options and filters as in Hallast et al.\ \cite{Hallast2026}.

Because the Hallast annotation did not include DYZ17, we annotated it separately. We built a profile hidden Markov model (HMM) from start-to-start repeat units in 140 assemblies using MAFFT \cite{Katoh2013} and hmmbuild, then generated a 5,573-bp consensus sequence with hmmemit \cite{Eddy2011}. We searched each assembly for the consensus with nhmmer, joined hits less than 10 kb apart into blocks and measured DYZ17 length from the first to the last hit of the longest block. For Yq12 and its components, we used the Hallast annotation for reference lengths and our own profile-HMM annotation to select markers.

\subsection{Marker selection}\label{sec:m-markers}
For each satellite target or component, we counted canonical 31-mers in the corresponding regions of the pangenome assemblies using KMC \cite{Kokot2017} to identify candidate markers. For each target or component, a candidate marker had to occur in at least a fraction $p$ of the training assemblies and outside it in fewer than a fraction $o$ of those assemblies. For $o=0$, the marker had to be absent outside the target or component. They also had to be absent from both the non-Y sequence of CHM13 \cite{Nurk2022} and 71 HPRC release 2 haplotypes. These were selected from assemblies with at least ten telomere-to-telomere chromosomes, excluding two haplotypes with elevated Y-satellite marker counts and haplotypes from study individuals or their relatives. We tested $p\in\{0.50,0.60,0.70,0.80,0.90,0.95\}$ and $o\in\{0,0.05,0.10,0.25\}$ using 5-fold cross-validation within each training partition. Families were kept in the same fold. For each combination, we trained the model on four folds and measured prediction errors on the fifth, rotating the held-out fold. We selected the values for each marker set from these evaluations.

To normalize satellite counts, we selected single-copy Y markers from X-degenerate regions. Each marker had to be present in at least 90\% of the training assemblies. In 149 assemblies, a median of 99.49\% of the markers occurred once on the Y chromosome.

Sequencing errors in non-Y reads can produce sequences that match a marker. To reduce this background, we screened all marker sets against CHM13 without chrY and the 71 HPRC release 2 haplotypes described above. For each marker, we counted 31-mers differing by one base in each assembly and excluded markers whose median count across the 72 assemblies exceeded ten.

The released model contains 5,072 DYZ3, 279,354 DYZ1, 20,979 DYZ2, 29,931 DYZ18S, 92,888 DYZ19, 58,498 DYZ17 and 19,344 Yq12-residual markers, 6,584,766 single-copy Y markers and 378,138 markers of an autosomal window at chr8:91--101 Mb (Autosomal window). It also counts 612,019 markers of the whole Yq12, which are not used to estimate its length. The marker sets are stored as masked superstrings \cite{Sladky2023}.

\subsection{Autosomal window}\label{sec:m-window}
To estimate Y dosage, Yruler compares the single-copy Y marker count with that of an autosomal region chosen for stable copy number. We evaluated 2,503 windows in GRCh38. We aligned 465 HPRC release 2 haplotypes to GRCh38 with minimap2 (\texttt{-x asm20 -{}-eqx -c}) \cite{Li2018} and used IMPG \cite{IMPG} to build an implicit pangenome graph and project each window into the haplotypes. We ranked windows by six criteria, giving equal weight to their ranks: sequence conservation in the least-conserved superpopulation, the proportion of 31-mers with a single copy across superpopulations, copy-number variation among the males with reference assemblies, differences in median copy number between superpopulations, agreement of Y dosage across 42 sequencing runs from 17 males sequenced more than once and similarity in G+C content to the single-copy Y markers. The 17 males included 3 from Genome in a Bottle and 4 from the Platinum Pedigree, sequenced with Element and Illumina (2 Platinum males also had 1kGP data), and 10 sequenced by both 1kGP and SGDP. We selected chr8:91--101 Mb, which balanced these criteria.

We selected markers from the window's projections in the 101 HPRC haplotypes that met the assembly-completeness criterion. The window is used for Y dosage, whereas satellite lengths are normalized by the single-copy Y markers. Candidate 31-mers had to occur in at least 90\% of these haplotypes and at one copy in at least 95\% of the haplotypes in every superpopulation. Of 450,821 candidates, 386,153 passed these criteria; the additional non-Y similarity filter described above retained 378,138.

\subsection{Read counting}\label{sec:m-counting}
Yruler reads BAM, CRAM, SAM, FASTQ or FASTA files through HTSlib \cite{Bonfield2021}, including unmapped reads. Indexed BAM and CRAM files are read in parallel. Secondary and supplementary records are skipped. Reads marked as duplicates are retained, and no mapping-quality or base-quality filter is applied. Each occurrence of a 31-mer matching a marker adds one to that marker's count. Yruler writes per-marker counts, counts summed by marker set, and satellite length and Y dosage estimates.

\subsection{G+C correction}\label{sec:m-gc}
Sequencing libraries can recover reads at different rates depending on their G+C content, affecting satellite estimates. For each sample, Yruler builds a correction curve from the single-copy Y markers. To limit the influence of unusually high counts, markers with counts above $10\times(\mathrm{mean}+1)$, where the mean is over all single-copy Y markers, are omitted when building the curve.

We grouped the single-copy Y markers by the number of G or C bases in each 31-mer (0--31). For each group, or G+C bin, relative depth is the mean count of the retained markers divided by the overall mean count of the retained markers. Building the curve requires at least 1,000,000 single-copy Y marker counts. A G+C bin must contain at least 1,000 markers and 10,000 retained counts; bins that do not meet these requirements use the value of the nearest qualifying G+C bin. Each satellite marker count is divided by the relative depth for its G+C content, and the resulting sum is multiplied by the mean relative depth across the single-copy Y markers. If no curve can be built, Yruler uses the raw counts. A G+C correction curve was obtained for all samples with detectable Y in the three cohorts.

DYZ2 is rich in A and T, and its markers with few G or C bases gave the largest differences between libraries of the same male. We therefore required DYZ2 markers to contain at least 14 G or C bases out of 31. This threshold was chosen using 10 males sequenced by both SGDP and 1kGP, without using their assembled lengths.

\subsection{Length estimation}\label{sec:m-length}
For sample $i$ and marker set $c$, corresponding to a satellite target or component, we divided the sum of G+C-corrected marker counts, $S^{\mathrm{GC}}_{ic}$, by the sum of the single-copy Y marker counts, $S_{iY}$. A factor $K_c$ converts this ratio into base pairs. For each target or component, $T_c$ is the set of training samples with assembled length $L_{jc}$:
\begin{equation}
 K_c=\frac{\sum_{j\in T_c}L_{jc}}{\sum_{j\in T_c}S^{\mathrm{GC}}_{jc}/S_{jY}},\qquad \widehat L_{ic}=K_c\,\frac{S^{\mathrm{GC}}_{ic}}{S_{iY}}.
\end{equation}

We estimated Yq12 length as the sum of DYZ1, DYZ2, DYZ18S and the remaining Yq12 sequence, which has its own markers and scale factor. The estimates of DYZ1, DYZ2, DYZ18S and the remaining sequence provide the repeat composition of Yq12.

To estimate the DYZ1:DYZ2 ratio in repeat units, we divided each estimated length by the corresponding median annotated unit length: 3,478.9 bp for DYZ1 and 2,404.6 bp for DYZ2, calculated from the 91 reference assemblies \cite{Hallast2026}. Across these assemblies, the mean annotated unit lengths per assembly ranged from 3,441 to 3,509 bp for DYZ1 and from 2,395 to 2,417 bp for DYZ2. Using lineage-specific medians for lineages with at least three assemblies would change the unit ratio by at most 0.39\%.

\subsection{Y dosage and Y state}\label{sec:m-dosage}
For sample $i$, Y dosage compares the mean count per single-copy Y marker with the mean count per autosomal marker:
\begin{equation}
 D_i=\frac{2}{s}\times\frac{S_{iY}/n_Y}{S_{iA}/n_A},
\end{equation}
where $S_{iY}$ and $S_{iA}$ are the sums of marker counts for single-copy Y regions and the chr8:91--101 Mb window, respectively, and $n_Y$ and $n_A$ are their numbers of markers. The scale factor $s=0.9596$ is the median unscaled dosage $2\times(S_{iY}/n_Y)/(S_{iA}/n_A)$ among 1,595 1kGP samples with dosage above half the cohort median.

Yruler reports no lengths for $D_i\le0.025$, although marker counts are retained. The highest Y dosage among 346 females from the three cohorts (208 from 1kGP, 44 from HGDP and 94 from SGDP) was 0.0153 in HGDP00760, below the threshold and between the medians of simulated mixtures with male read fractions of 0.01 and 0.02 (0.0103 and 0.0205 at 5$\times$; Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:no_y_cut}a,b). Below the threshold, marker counts may still contain background signal.


To test low Y abundance, we mixed reads from 6 male--female pairs from 1kGP at male read fractions of 0.01, 0.02, 0.1, 0.5 and 1, and included female reads alone. We analysed mixtures at 5 and 1$\times$ autosomal depth, with 3 independent random downsampling replicates at each depth (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:no_y_cut}). These mixtures approximate samples containing cells with and without a Y chromosome. At 5$\times$, the median absolute difference between measured and expected Y dosage (the male read fraction multiplied by the male's dosage) ranged from \textbf{[minimum]} to \textbf{[maximum]} across fractions, with a maximum individual difference of \textbf{[maximum]}. At low male fractions, the measured dosage exceeded the expected value because of female background (median \textbf{[dosage]} in female-only mixtures). At a male fraction of 0.1, the median change in estimated length compared with male-only reads was within \textbf{[percentage]} for each target (the largest change was \textbf{[percentage]} for \textbf{[target]}). In a separate calculation without the reporting cutoff, the median change reached \textbf{[percentage]} for \textbf{[target]} at a male fraction of 0.01. % Update mixture results and verify their direction using the current model.

To screen for unusual Y profiles, we summed single-copy Y marker counts separately over Yp (X-degenerate blocks 1--2) and proximal Yq (blocks 3--8). Each marker was assigned to a block if it occurred there in at least 90\% of the Y assemblies containing it. For a more detailed profile, we counted chrY 31-mers in 100-kb CHM13v2 windows, retaining those absent from other CHM13 sequences. Both profiles were normalized to the chr8 window and the median of males without flagged Y profiles (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:chrY_unusual_males}). PAR2 gains were assessed using \textbf{[markers and normalization to be confirmed]}. % Update after the pangenome-based PAR2 check.

Samples were flagged, in this order, for Yp depletion (Yp below 0.5, Yq within 0.1 of one), Yq loss (Yp within 0.1 of one and Yq more than 0.1 below Yp) and arm imbalance (Yp and Yq more than 0.1 apart). Subsequent categories were PAR2 gain (PAR2 dose at least 1.25 with PAR1 within 0.1 of one), Y gain (dosage at least 1.75), intermediate gain (1.25 to 1.75) or low dosage (below 0.9). These flags are screening categories, not confirmed karyotypes. All males with Y estimates, including those with flagged profiles, were included in population summaries. The per-male table marks estimates outside the range of the evaluated assemblies (135 of 1,597 males for at least one target).

\subsection{Accuracy evaluation}\label{sec:m-benchmark}
Accuracy was measured with ten repeats of 5-fold cross-validation, with individuals of the same recorded family kept in the same fold. Markers, thresholds and $K_c$ were fitted on the four training folds only. Array lengths were predicted for individuals in the held-out fold. The error is $100 \times |\widehat L_i/L_i-1|$ (Table~\ref{tab:accuracy}).

In a second test, each of 18 broad Y lineages was excluded in turn from training, together with any family shared with test individuals, and predicted from the remaining references (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:lineage_validation}). For depth, test reads were thinned to 20, 15, 10, 5, 2 and 1$\times$ base coverage while training reads kept full depth (Fig.~\ref{fig:method}e, and Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:y_dosage}a for Y dosage).

Parent--offspring concordance used the 309 pairs of the recorded 1000 Genomes pedigree among males with estimates. Of these, 282 pairs have the parent in the original 2,504-sample release and the offspring in the 698-sample extension, so they also compare sequencing batches (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:pedigrees}). Y specificity used 243 female genomes held out from marker selection, with the single-copy Y marker count replaced by its expected sum at the female haploid depth.

We also evaluated four external datasets (HG002, HG003, HG005 and an Element library), held out from model choices; median errors were 0.79\% for DYZ3, 1.09\% for DYZ17 and 2.57\% for DYZ19 (3 males each). As a baseline, we scaled aligned read depth from BWA and DRAGEN by the depth in chrY:14.5--15.5 Mb and fitted one factor per target on the training males of each fold. In a further test, we trained on males from the original 2,504-sample release and tested on those from the 698-sample extension. The aligned-depth and batch test results are in \texttt{results/checks/d7\_aligned\_depth\_baseline.tsv} and \texttt{results/checks/d9\_batch\_holdout.tsv}.

Three J2a1a1 males with short DYZ19 arrays were evaluated in cross-validation, and a fourth was held out from training (Fig.~\ref{fig:method}c). DYZ18S shares 4,720 of its 29,931 31-mers with DYZ1 (Table~\ref{tab:accuracy}).

We also checked DYZ19 lengths in six J2a1a1 males outside the training set using assemblies from the 1000 Genomes nanopore resource \cite{Gustafson2024}. We aligned the 50 kb of GRCh38 sequence on each side of the array (chrY:20,004,500--20,054,500 and 20,351,000--20,401,000) to each assembly with minimap2 (\texttt{-x asm5}) and measured the distance between the inner ends of the flanks when both aligned in full, with mapping quality 60, to one contig. In two reference males, this distance in R10.4.1 assemblies was within 0.12\% of the reference array length; R9.4.1 assemblies were shorter, so we used only R10.4.1 assemblies. Four J2a1a1 males had arrays of 62,433--63,808 bp, and Yruler estimates differed by $-$1.2\% to +5.0\%. The fifth R10.4.1 assembly was broken in the array, and the sixth male had only an R9.4.1 assembly.

\subsection{Model choices}
The inner-loop score, DYZ2 G+C floor and autosomal window were fixed outside cross-validation. We chose mean error as the inner-loop score after comparing it with the median and 90th percentile. For Yq12 and its components, we compared markers from published blocks with markers from our HMM annotation and retained the latter, which gave lower DYZ18S errors. The DYZ2 G+C floor was chosen using 10 males sequenced by both SGDP and 1kGP, without assembly lengths.

\subsection{Library effects}
The short-read model was evaluated on PCR-free 150-bp 1kGP libraries. In 10 males sequenced by both SGDP (PCR-free, 100-bp reads) and 1kGP, DYZ19 estimates were higher in SGDP, including after cutting the 1kGP reads to 100 bases. In 4 males sequenced PCR-free by both HGDP and SGDP, DYZ2 estimates were 21.3\% higher in SGDP. Across 13 HGDP populations, DYZ2 estimates from PCR libraries were 45.7\% lower than from PCR-free libraries. Population analyses therefore use PCR-free libraries and compare lengths within cohorts.

HGDP libraries were labelled PCR-free when their CRAM file had at least two read groups. This rule matched the published library type \cite{Bergstrom2020} for 533 of the 535 HGDP files with one (397 PCR-free, 136 PCR) and labelled the other two, which are PCR-free, as PCR. SGDP libraries were labelled PCR-free when they came from LP6005 plates, and ENA records all 167 of these with a matching record as PCR-free.

\subsection{Comparison with depth-based estimates}\label{sec:m-sanroman}
Earlier short-read studies measured DYZ3 in 366 males \cite{Miga2014} and DYZ1 in 396 \cite{Altemose2014}, and used DYZ1 read depth as a proxy for Yq12 length in 1,225 males \cite{SanRoman2024}. These estimates could not be scaled or tested against complete arrays from many individuals.

San Roman et al.\ \cite{SanRoman2024} used read depth in a DYZ1 region (chrY:56,676,260--56,771,430) as a proxy for Yq12 length in relative units. They used bowtie2 alignments to hg38, corrected depth for G+C content as in Teitz et al.\ \cite{Teitz2018} and normalized it by depth in chrY:14.5--15.5 Mb. We ran their scripts (\texttt{Get\_DYZ1\_Depth.R}, commit 4dd8a71, and \texttt{calculate\_gc\_curve.py}, \texttt{unbias\_gc.py} and \texttt{find\_unique\_regions.py}, commit 9b23bce) on the 1000 Genomes 30x PCR-free alignments of the Burrows--Wheeler Aligner (BWA-MEM) of the 126 males with at least one assembled array. We adapted the scripts for CRAM input, input paths and parallel execution. We also adjusted the read count that scales the G+C curve, skipped reads whose mate could not be read, and enabled the third step of the mappability script as written.

The comparison used 83 males with an assembled DYZ1 array and Yruler cross-validation estimates (82 for Yq12 and DYZ2). For each split, we fitted a scale factor on the training males by dividing their summed assembled lengths by their summed depths. We applied this factor to the test males in 10 repeats of 5-fold cross-validation grouped by family, as for Yruler, and compared the two methods on the same males. Errors are absolute percentages of the assembled length \cite{Hallast2026}, and P values are from two-sided paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on per-male mean absolute errors.

For DYZ1, the methods had similar errors (median 1.65\% for depth and 2.27\% for Yruler; P = 0.69). For Yq12, median errors were 4.15\% and 1.70\%, respectively (P = $6.3\times10^{-9}$). We also assessed DYZ1 depth as a proxy for DYZ2 length; individual results are in \texttt{results/model/sanroman\_individual\_summary.tsv}.

To test whether sequence divergence in a lineage lowers the marker counts, we compared the estimates with aligned read depth, which tolerates mismatches and uses no Yruler marker. Reads per base in the GRCh38 DYZ3 block (chrY:10,083,500--10,621,500) and in the DYZ19 core (chrY:20,054,500--20,351,000) were divided by reads per base in chrY:14.5--15.5 Mb (Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:markerfree}a,b). Aligned depth depends on the aligner: BWA and DRAGEN gave different depths on the same reads, whereas Yruler counts were identical (Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:markerfree}c).

\subsection{Runtime and memory}\label{sec:m-cost}
For Fig.~\ref{fig:method}d, the released model was run on the 130 local 1000 Genomes CRAM files of males with reference assemblies (median 16.2 GB, median 31-mer depth 29.1$\times$) with 8 threads on 16-core cluster nodes, reading each CRAM directly, and timed with GNU time. Median wall-clock time was 106 s (10th--90th percentile 95--130 s, maximum 231 s), median CPU time 1,022 s and median peak memory 3.77 GB (3.76--3.79 GB). We also measured runtime and memory at 1, 4, 8 and 16 threads and at lower coverage on three files; results are in \texttt{results/checks/runtime\_threads\_coverage.tsv}.

\subsection{Nanopore reads}\label{sec:m-ont}
We evaluated a separate nanopore model using ten repeats of family-grouped 5-fold cross-validation on HPRC R10.4.1 reads (dorado super-accuracy base calls) \cite{HPRCRelease2} from 82 males with assembled arrays. Markers, thresholds and scale factors were selected within each training partition. Medians of per-male mean absolute percentage errors ranged from 4.5\% to 9.8\% across the seven targets (Table~\ref{tab:ont}). The final nanopore model was then fitted using all 82 males. The reference assemblies were built from PacBio HiFi and ultra-long nanopore reads of the same individuals \cite{Hallast2026,HPRCRelease2}, so these errors may be optimistic.

The short-read and nanopore models were trained separately. The nanopore marker and model files are in the \texttt{data/ont} folder of the Yruler repository.

We compared estimates from the final nanopore and short-read models in the 82 males used to train the nanopore model (Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:markerfree}d). Absolute relative differences were calculated as $100|L_{\mathrm{ONT}}/L_{\mathrm{short}}-1|$. For DYZ3, Spearman $\rho$ was 0.972 and the median absolute relative difference was 6.9\%. Nanopore estimates also recovered the DYZ3 contrasts in R1b and O2a and short DYZ19 arrays in J2a1a1, although the O2a and J2a1a1 comparisons involved few males.

\subsection{Cohorts, lineages and tree}\label{sec:m-population}
We applied the released model to 1,639 1kGP high-coverage samples \cite{ByrskaBishop2022}: 1,597 of 1,598 males had estimates and entered population summaries. Replication used 537 PCR-free HGDP and SGDP males \cite{Bergstrom2020,Mallick2016}, including 24 SGDP males without recorded sex assigned XY by X and Y dosage \textbf{[verify X dosage]}. Recorded sex came from the corrected 1kGP pedigree and HGDP and SGDP sample records in ENA; genetic sex assigned by SGDP agreed with our Y dosage in all 172 assigned SGDP samples. Haplogroups were called from chrY genotypes with HaploGrouper using the International Society of Genetic Genealogy (ISOGG) 2019 tree. HGDP and SGDP calls agreed with published lineages \cite{Hallast2021} in all 662 males with both. Haplogroups describe paternal lineages, not genome-wide ancestry. Superpopulation labels in Fig.~\ref{fig:biology}a are from 1kGP metadata; cohort locations are shown in Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:world_map}.

The tree of Fig.~\ref{fig:biology}a follows the procedure of Hallast et al.\ \cite{Hallast2026}. Variants were called with BCFtools 1.16 inside the mask of Y regions accessible to short reads \cite{Poznik2013}. Calls with depth below 3 or allele fraction below 85\% were set to missing, and sites with more than 5\% missing calls removed. RAxML 8.2.10 built one maximum-likelihood tree (GTRGAMMA, 100 rapid bootstraps) of the 1,597 males, rooted between the three A0 males and the rest. Clades were collapsed for display, and no topology or order used satellite lengths. For Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:J_DYZ19}, clades were collapsed by the first seven characters of the ISOGG 2019 call.

\subsection{Statistics}\label{sec:m-stats}
Population summaries are medians and interquartile ranges per lineage. We tested differences across the 14 lineages with at least 20 males using Kruskal--Wallis tests and tie-corrected Dunn tests with Holm correction for pairwise comparisons. The share of variance accounted for by lineage was calculated as one minus the within-lineage sum of squares divided by the total sum of squares. These analyses used 1,248 males, one per kinship group. Two-group contrasts used two-sided Mann--Whitney tests within each cohort; 1kGP tests used one male per kinship group. We repeated the main tests 1,000 times, randomly choosing one male per group, to assess sensitivity to this choice. HGDP, SGDP and 1kGP were analysed separately.

In 1kGP, kinship groups join males connected directly or through other males by first- or second-degree KING-robust kinship \cite{Manichaikul2010} in the PLINK 2 resource \cite{Chang2015} or by kinship above 0.05 in the gnomAD relatedness table \cite{Koenig2024}. We kept one male per group, the first by sample name. In HGDP and SGDP, we used gnomAD kinship above 0.05 and KING estimates for additional SGDP males; three SGDP males had no kinship data. HGDP and SGDP P values use all qualifying males. We repeated the tests with one male per kinship group to assess sensitivity to relatedness; detailed results are in \texttt{results/checks/relatedness.tsv}.

Confidence intervals for lineage medians were obtained by resampling kinship groups. For Fig.~\ref{fig:biology}f, log DYZ1 and log DYZ2 lengths were centred within broad lineages in 1,194 1kGP males without a reference assembly, one per kinship group. The ratio of their standard deviations has a bootstrap 95\% interval and was tested with the Pitman--Morgan test. To assess the contribution of DYZ2 variation to the association between Yq12 length and DYZ1 fraction, we fitted an ordinary least-squares slope with both quantities centred within lineages in the same males. We permuted DYZ2 lengths among males within each lineage 10,000 times, keeping DYZ1 and the rest of Yq12 fixed, and recalculated Yq12 length and DYZ1 fraction for each permutation. The observed slope was $-$1.73 percentage points per 10 Mb (95\% bootstrap interval $-$2.09 to $-$1.34; 2,000 within-lineage resamples), compared with a median of $-$0.65 (central 95\% permutation interval $-$0.84 to $-$0.45).

As a phylogenetic sensitivity analysis, we fitted phylogenetic generalized least-squares models on the 1kGP single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) tree, with Pagel's $\lambda$ estimated by maximum likelihood. Results for individual lineage comparisons, pairwise tests and phylogenetic models are in \texttt{results/model/biology\_tests.tsv}, \texttt{dunn\_pairs.tsv} and \texttt{pgls\_composition.tsv}, respectively.

All reported P values are two-sided where applicable. No statistical method was used to predetermine sample sizes: every male with reads and the required references or estimates was included. Cross-validation folds assign family groups at random, with fixed seeds. The study analysed public data and used no blinding.
